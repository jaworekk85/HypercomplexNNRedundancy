**Short abstract:**

Quaternion neural networks offer structured weight sharing and can reduce the
number of trainable parameters, but the effects of additional structural
compression on their accuracy and representations are not yet fully understood.
We investigate channel pruning and compensation in compact quaternion networks,
comparing reconstruction of removed features with direct reconstruction of the
surviving layer outputs. The comparison preserves quaternion weight structure and
the size of the compressed architecture, and includes conventional real-valued
controls. Our experiments cover CIFAR-10 and EuroSAT, with a residual architecture
also considered for CIFAR-10. Preliminary results indicate that output-aware
reconstruction can improve quality immediately after pruning, although the
differences can become smaller after fine-tuning. We also plan to investigate
layer-dependent structural choices under a common parameter budget. The study
aims to clarify when structured compensation is useful and where its limitations
appear, rather than assume an intrinsic advantage of quaternion structure.
